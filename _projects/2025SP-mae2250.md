During spring of my sophomore year, the Introduction to Mechanical Design class I took had us work in groups to gain hands on prototyping and design experience. We went from a generalized problem with many solutions to a specific subproblem with two iterations of prototypes. 

## The Problem

Inspired by Ithaca in winter, my group decided to focus on improving the walking experience in bad weather, be it storms, cold, rain, or snow. After conducting interviews of people who live in cold and rainy environments to learn about their commuting habits and how they stay safe and dry in suboptimal conditions, we brainstormed a host of ideas from shoe attachments that would improve friction to umbrellas small enough to attach to backpacks. 

![Image of umbrella rib enforcer mockup]({{ "/assets/images/ribenforcer.png" | relative_url }}){: .inline-image-l}

We created rough mockups of our most promising ideas, including a set of rib enforcing rods for an umbrella, built and designed by myself. The idea was to create an object that could be easily attached to the shaft of an umbrella and had arms with hooks that connected to the ribs to help prevent an umbrella from flipping inside out in strong winds. While this was not the project my group decided to pursue, I do plan on one day returning to the idea and creating a more functional prototype to test its feasibility. 

Ultimately, we chose to design the Keychain Umbrella. Our goal: to create an umbrella small enough to clip onto a backpack or purse while also being strong enough to avoid turning inside out in strong winds. 

## Our Goals
Knowing we only had two prototype iterations, we sought to set realistic and testable goals for ourselves. 

- Prototype should weight under 1.5 lbs
- Prototype should be waterproof
- Prototype should be able to be held above head without collapsing
- Prototype should be less than 6 inches long when folded

## The First Prototype
![Image of first umbrella prototype]({{ "/assets/images/prototype1.png" | relative_url }}){: .inline-image-l}

The goal with our first prototype was to create something the rough shape and size of what we envisioned the umbrella to be. Using tension rods, a 3D printed connector, aluminum bars, and a tarp we created a rough "looks like" prototype. 

In creating our first prototype, we discovered a few issues. While the tarp we had was waterproof, it was too bulky to be used as the fabric for our umbrella. The tension rods we ordered were longer than our 6 inch requirement, though we detirmined this to not be a major issue as potential future prototypes would have a custom designed shaft. Our ribs were also held together with screws and lacked a mechanism to allow for easy opening and shutting of the umbrella. 

After looking over the problems with our first prototype, we decided it would be best to design our own linkages for our next iteration and laser cut them out of acrylic to allow for easier manufacturing than using the aluminum bars we had.

## The Final Prototype

![Image of umbrella CAD model]({{ "/assets/images/final.png" | relative_url }}){: .inline-image-l}

For the final prototype, we completely redesigned the ribs to be a set of linkages to allow it to fold and unfold easily and hold its shape. Using Fusion 360, I designed and tested the ribs, making sure they would both work properly and fit within our size goals. We laser cut them from acrylic to ensure they were lightweight and quick to make, and 3D printed parts to connect it to the tension rods.  

We also updated the fabric from a thick, waterproof tarp, to a wax soaked cotton. While the cotton was thinner and thus easier to fold and handle, the wax made it sticky and it collected dirt easily. 

![Image of umbrella rib enforcer mockup]({{ "/assets/images/demo.png" | relative_url }}){: .inline-image-l}

Our final design was a significant improvement over the previous prototype. We were able to create a working umbrella that satisfied our design goals, though there are improvements to be made. It even managed to keep me dry during a rainstorm!

## Future Improvements

While we were satisfied with the design we created over the course of two prototypes, there are definitely improvements to be made. 

#### Ribs

While the umbrella ribs fit within our 6 inch height limit when folded, they are rather wide. Future changes to the material and linkage design need to be done in order to decrease the folded width of the design.

The diameter of the umbrella needs to be increased. The current design is barely wide enough to fit a person under, and would be unable to protect any bags or other items the user has. The rib design needs to be adjusted to increase the diameter of the umbrella when expanded while also not increasing the folded width.
