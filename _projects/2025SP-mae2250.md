During spring of my sophomore year, the Introduction to Mechanical Design class I took had us work in groups to gain hands on prototyping and design experience. We went from a generalized problem with many solutions to a specific subproblem with two iterations of prototypes. 

## The Problem

Inspired by Ithaca in winter, my group decided to focus on improving the walking experience in bad weather, be it storms, cold, rain, or snow. After conducting interviews of people who live in cold and rainy environments to learn about their commuting habits and how they stay safe and dry in suboptimal conditions, we brainstormed a host of ideas from shoe attachments that would improve friction to umbrellas small enough to attach to backpacks. 

![Image of umbrella rib enforcer mockup]({{ "/assets/images/ribenforcer.png" | relative_url }}){: .inline-image-l}

We created rough mockups of our most promising ideas, including a set of rib enforcing rods for an umbrella, built and designed by myself. The idea was to create an object that could be easily attached to the shaft of an umbrella and had arms with hooks that connected to the ribs to help prevent an umbrella from flipping inside out in strong winds. While this was not the project my group decided to pursue, I do plan on one day returning to the idea and creating a more functional prototype to test its feasibility.
